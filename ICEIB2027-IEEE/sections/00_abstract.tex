\begin{abstract}
Mountain motorcycle touring requires coordinated decisions about travel time, weather, road accessibility, daylight, and fuel availability. Conventional navigation estimates do not explicitly account for individual riding pace or propagate travel-time uncertainty into these constraints. This paper proposes Moto Touring Copilot, a pre-trip decision-support framework using 279 historical GPX trips from a single rider. The proposed approach combines segment-level LightGBM quantile regression, trip-level conformal calibration, arrival-time-aware environmental queries, and a multi-agent planning architecture with deterministic verification. Candidate itineraries are checked against daylight, fuel range, station opening hours, weather warnings, road restrictions, and numerical traceability. An evaluation protocol compares personalized predictions with conventional baselines and examines planning feasibility using an independent oracle. The framework is currently at the proposal stage; experimental outcomes remain to be established.
\end{abstract}
